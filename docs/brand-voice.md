# @gamedevaitools brand voice and X playbook

Research date: 2026-10-17 (AEST). Sources: public X API reads (the `x` connector: user lookups, recent timelines, full-archive search) and the repo. Metrics are as of the read and will drift. L = likes, R = reposts, Re = replies, I = impressions. **Everything below is real data unless it's marked _unknown_ or _draft_.** Nothing was posted and no one was contacted.

Data limits, so you know what to trust:
- The `search_users` endpoint returned 403 (app-only auth isn't allowed). Competitors were found by exact-handle lookups and post search, so there will be accounts I missed.
- @TheOnion's timeline call returned only 13–15 Jan 2026 posts (25 posts, about one every 30 min). The patterns hold, but the sample is old.
- @MoonPie's last 24 own posts span 3 Apr–24 Sep 2026. They post rarely.
- @duolingo's last 25 posts all fall within 15–16 Oct 2026, and nearly all are replies.
- These handles were not found on X: meshy_ai, aiforgamedev, AIGameDevNews. @80Level (265,892 followers) was profiled but its posts weren't pulled, so its performance is _unknown_.

**Gaps we can exploit**
1. **No one combines game-dev AI tools with personality.** The directories post ad copy that gets single-digit likes even at 35k followers. A funny, useful account starts with almost no competition.
2. **No one covers licences, commercial use or Steam disclosure on X**, which is exactly what our site sources and dates. Disclosure is a live fight (see C).
3. **Free, useful drops win** (Scenario's L1,442 plugin post). Our equivalent is a free resource: a checklist, a comparison, a "which tool is safe to ship" thread.
4. **The dedicated game-dev AI handles are dead** (@AiGameDev, @GamedevAi). Only the 2-day-old @Tool_Meta overlaps, with 4 followers.
5. **Comment-bait works even for bland accounts** (TAAFT Re33). Our version could be "describe your worst AI asset fail in 5 words".

## C) What's resonating in AI game dev now (searches over 3–17 Oct 2026)

- **"Is this vibe-coded?" is an accusation format.** [@ZypherHQ "Is this a vibe-coded game?"](https://x.com/ZypherHQ/status/2109640539502948627): L137 Re65. Replies run far ahead of likes, so it's divisive. "vibe coded game" is used as an insult throughout the replies.
- **Ambitious showcase stories travel.** [Little Prince 3D narrative game built over 16 days with Opus 5.5 + Tripo](https://x.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2110177518292779081): L76.
- **Generative-AI disclosure backlash is the loudest topic.** The highest-liked posts in the "uses generative AI" search are negative ([L84](https://x.com/dynaviette/status/2107443024683036736), [L76](https://x.com/Yhug_Yug/status/2109028691485569504)). Aion 2 is a flashpoint: [it hit 397,905 concurrent players and sits at Mixed reviews while openly using gen-AI assets](https://x.com/playswave_com/status/2108260303020626365). Some players are hiding AI-disclosed games on Steam ([example](https://x.com/bartmaniaque/status/2107530180290036126)). Asset buyers don't know store assets can trigger disclosure ([example](https://x.com/Ts1nda/status/2111056756579106933)).
- **The "slop" culture war.** [@IndieGatsu: "unless you create everything from front to back yourself, it's slop… delusional"](https://x.com/IndieGatsu/status/2109392576466079941). Many devs hedge with "AI for prototyping, clean up by hand" ([example](https://x.com/DomDoesDev_/status/2111103164174963088)).
- **Image-to-3D reality checks.** Practitioners ask about formats, cleanup and topology rather than previews ([SumeruAI's four questions](https://x.com/SumeruAI/status/2111112846385758562); ["test one asset end to end before wiring the MCP"](https://x.com/catmanyau/status/2111243625338122412); ["we ran the same car through four image-to-3D pipelines… one wrecked the car"](https://x.com/fightbetscity/status/2109313761475436871)).
- **Consistency pain.** ["extremely inconsistent. Even when you correct the AI…"](https://x.com/classicstevet/status/2110205429821149397) about AI game art.
- **Prompt-to-game launches** (Unity Spark, Google's vibe-coding game tool) drive "will it be fun or just fast?" takes ([example](https://x.com/samihd98/status/2107872037347938815)).

Pain points to anchor jokes in: topology and retopo cleanup, inconsistent characters across generations, agents losing context, prompt-to-game demos versus shipping, disclosure anxiety, licence and commercial-use confusion, the "slop" label landing on honest devs, and hardware or setup hell for local models.

**Tone warning:** the community is split and hostile to hype. We punch at hype, demos and vendor claims. We never punch at artists worried about their jobs or at devs who use AI. Steer clear of taking sides in the culture war; be the honest, informed, funny friend.

## D) Synthesis

### Top 5 insights
1. **Reach comes from moments and replies, not the feed.** MoonPie's best post rode the Artemis moment (I483k vs a typical 10–25k), and Duolingo's biggest post was a reply in someone else's thread (I1.86M). Our moments are model launches, engine announcements, Steam policy changes and viral "is this vibe-coded?" posts.
2. **Shorter and plainer wins.** The top performers are one sentence, lowercase or Title-Case headlines, with no hashtags or links in the body. The Onion's four-word "Zillow Adds Segregation Score" got L1,473.
3. **The AI-directory accounts are failing at X.** TAAFT (35k followers) peaks at L23, Futurepedia at L5. Ad-copy threads die, so a voice is a real moat.
4. **Usefulness that's free gets the real numbers.** Scenario's open-sourced plugin (L1,442) beat everything else in the niche by roughly 20×. Pair the jokes with real drops.
5. **Disclosure, licences and "slop" are the hot nerve,** and it's the exact data our directory holds (commercial-use terms, sourced and dated). Be the calm, funny, accurate account in that fight.

### Voice guide

**We are:** a slightly haunted directory that has met every AI tool personally and has feelings about all of them. Deadpan, sincere, tired, kind to devs.

**Tone sliders (0–10):**
- Absurd ← 7 → literal
- Deadpan ← 8 → excitable
- Kind to users ← 10 → snarky to users (we never move off kind)
- Snark at hype ← 7 → neutral
- Useful ← 5 → pure bit (alternate between the two)
- Corporate ← 0

**Do**
- Write tools as characters with feelings ("the 3D generator is going through something").
- Use lowercase for personality posts and Title Case for fake headlines.
- Keep it to one idea per post, about 140 characters ideal, no hashtags, at most one emoji.
- Put links in a reply, not the main post (MoonPie and Duolingo keep text clean).
- Every few posts, add real value: a dated fact, a licence gotcha, a tool comparison with a source.
- Admit our own limits ("our directory is also just a list made by someone tired").

**Don't**
- Mock artists, beginners, or anyone's game.
- Invent stats, fake quotes from real people, or fake screenshots of real accounts.
- Make claims that defame a named tool ("X steals art"). Joke about tool categories, or about claims a vendor actually made.
- Write "🚀 Introducing…", "game-changer", or thread-bro hooks.
- Engage in political or culture-war dunking. Don't quote-tweet a dev to dunk on them.

### Recurring formats (bits)
1. **Tool characters:** recurring personas such as *the 3D Generator* (proud of meshes with 2 million triangles), *the Agent* (forgets everything, apologises beautifully), *the Upscaler* (insists it's "enhancing"), *the Image Model* (gives your hero a new face every time), and *the Demo* (has never met production).
2. **Fake headlines (Onion):** "Report: …", "Local Indie Dev…", "Area Model…".
3. **Patch notes for my sanity:** a weekly bullet list styled as patch notes, about the week in AI game dev, with real items and a deadpan gloss.
4. **"it worked in the demo":** a series of demo-vs-reality one-liners.
5. **Licence Corner (useful):** one real commercial-use or disclosure fact, sourced and dated, linking to the directory.
6. **5-word pain prompt:** comment bait ("describe your worst AI asset in 5 words").
7. **Moment-jacking:** same-day takes on launches (models, engines, Steam).

### Weekly content mix and cadence (start at about 1–2 originals a day plus 5–15 replies)
- Mon: Patch notes for my sanity (news roundup, real links in replies).
- Tue: tool-character post plus a Licence Corner fact.
- Wed: fake headline plus a 5-word pain prompt.
- Thu: useful drop (a comparison, checklist or new directory page) in a plain voice with one joke line.
- Fri: "it worked in the demo" or a tool-character post.
- Weekend: one absurd or sincere MoonPie-style post, plus replies.
- Ratio: about 60% personality and 40% insight or news. Never more than one link-led post a day.
- Timing: our audience is US/EU devs, so schedule for about 14:00–17:00 UTC (00:00–03:00 AEST). Onion-style on-the-hour posting isn't needed at our size. _Measure and adjust after 4 weeks; we have no data of our own yet._

### Reply and engagement playbook
- **Reply-first growth (the Duolingo lesson):** spend more time replying than posting. Targets: viral AI game-dev demos, model and engine launches, dev complaints about tools. Add one funny line in persona or one useful fact (a licence or disclosure note).
- **On a dev's pain post:** empathise and joke at the tool, never at the dev. Offer a resource only if it really helps.
- **On hype posts ("built a whole game in ONE prompt"):** a gentle deadpan reality check about the demo, not the person.
- **On "is this AI slop?" fights:** stay out, or add a neutral fact (what Steam's disclosure actually requires, with a link). Never pile on.
- **Brand-to-brand (the Wendy's lesson):** playful replies to tool vendors' launches are fine, and affectionate is better than mean. No dunking on smaller accounts.
- **Fans:** reply to every early reply for the first months, MoonPie style: short, in character, sometimes inventing a lore character.
- **Corrections:** if a tool vendor or dev corrects a fact, thank them, fix it on the site, and say so publicly. That's part of our trust brand.
- **Never:** DM-baiting, engagement-farming giveaways, or arguing with angry users.

### Weekly one-up loop (monitor, then react)
1. **Mon (monitor, 30 min):** pull the last 7 days for @theresanaiforit, @futurepedia_io, @Tool_Meta, @Scenario_gg and @tripoai, plus searches for "vibe coded game", "image to 3D", "uses generative AI", "Steam AI disclosure" and "AI game dev". Note the top 3 posts by likes and replies.
2. **Diagnose:** what format worked (a free drop, a head-to-head, an accusation question)? What did competitors miss (licences, disclosure, honesty about limits)?
3. **React within 24h:** for each hot topic, post the version only we can make: funny plus sourced, and tied to a directory page.
4. **Ship one free useful thing a week** (a checklist, comparison or tier note with sources). That's the Scenario lesson.
5. **Review on Fri:** log what our posts got (likes, replies, profile visits, site clicks) and keep a simple sheet. Double down on formats that beat our median for two weeks running.

**Conclusions**
1. Media isn't a multiplier by itself. The directories put media on almost everything and still get 0–23 likes. Text-only posts top Wendy's, Duolingo's originals and several MoonPie and Onion posts.
2. Media wins when it **is** the content: a real moment (MoonPie and NASA), a real workflow demo (Scenario), a reaction GIF in a reply (Duolingo).
3. Stock promo images (store, product tour, sponsored) are the weakest media posts for every account studied.

**Our rules**
- **Default: text-only** for personality posts, fake headlines and patch notes. The joke should land without a picture.
- **Attach real screenshots** (not generated ones) when the image is the proof: a licence clause, a Steam disclosure field, a side-by-side of tool outputs we actually ran, a directory page. Crop tight, and keep any text readable on a phone.
- **Short screen recordings** (≤20 s, no music needed) for the weekly free useful drop, such as filtering the directory by "safe to ship commercially". That's the Scenario format at small scale.
- **Reaction images and GIFs** only in replies, and only from X's built-in GIF picker or our own art. Never use copyrighted meme templates of real people in a branded post.
- **Generated images (Higgsfield): sparingly, at most one a week.** Use them only when the joke is visual and the picture is honestly about AI output: a "tool character" portrait (the 3D Generator as a tired knight holding a 2-million-triangle sword), or a deliberately cursed "same character, 40 cousins" grid for the consistency bit. Always label the post or alt text as AI-generated. That's the honest move in a community fighting about disclosure, and it lets the image itself be the self-aware joke.
- **Never** use generated images to fake a real tool's output, a screenshot, a person, or a news event. Never use them as decoration on a news post.
- **Alt text on every image** (accessibility, and it can carry a second joke).
