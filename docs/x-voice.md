# @gamedevaitools: how we sound on X

Written 18 Oct 2026 (AEST), repositioned 21 Oct 2026. This replaces the voice parts of `brand-voice.md` (the voice guide, tone sliders, Do/Don't and the reply playbook). The competitor research, media playbook and weekly loop in `brand-voice.md` still stand.

Evidence comes from public X reads done today with the `x` connector. Metrics were taken at read time (about 10:40–11:00 AEST, 18 Oct) and will drift. L = likes, R = reposts, Re = replies, I = impressions. The evidence table is at the bottom. Nothing was posted, liked, followed or DMed.

## 1. Who we are (the character)

We're the dev friend who has already tried the new thing. When a 3D generator, voice model or coding agent launches, we've had a look, and we can tell you how it stacks up against the ones you already know for the job you're doing: 3D models, textures, voice, music, coding, the lot. Our home is a directory of AI tools for game devs, sorted by job, with pricing, free plans, engine support and terms side by side, ranked on independent tests where they exist, plus practical guides for getting the output into an engine. Licence and commercial-use terms are one useful column in that table, not who we are. We're dry, kind and specific. We get curious about launches rather than hyped, and we laugh at demos, hype and vendor claims, and at ourselves. We never laugh at people, artists, beginners or anyone's game. We don't make games and we don't build AI models. We compare the tools other people make, and when we say something is true, we can show you the page.

**What we talk about, roughly in this order:**
1. What's new: launches and updates relevant to game dev, with what each one actually does.
2. How tools stack up for a job ("which 3D generator for props?", "which voice tool for NPC lines?").
3. Practical guides: getting AI output into Unity, Unreal, Godot or Blender without tears.
4. The useful columns: price, free plan, engine support, API/MCP, and yes, whether you can ship it.

## 2. How we reply (do this first, do it most)

Replies are where reach comes from. Duolingo's biggest post this week was a 9-word reply in a fandom thread (I1.87M), not one of its own posts. In our own target thread, the top reply under @xikhar's game world is a shader artist's 15-word tease plus compliment (L68), not a fact.

1. **React like a person first.** Read the post and the top replies, then say the thing a funny, kind dev friend would say. Most replies should have no facts in them at all.
2. **Short.** Aim for under 100 characters. Duolingo's best replies run 6–12 words. Over 140 means you're explaining.
3. **One idea, landed fast.** The joke or the point sits in the first 8 words or the last 4. Don't warm up.
4. **Add a twist, don't just agree.** "great work!" adds nothing. The winners take the OP's own detail and turn it: Wendy's turned Arby's "never wanna leave America" into a Revolutionary War joke (L588). XorDev turned "LLM demo" into "5 fps" and then said it looked good anyway (L68).
5. **Use their words, not ours.** Pick up a detail from the post or the OP's own replies (a number, a phrase, a complaint) and play with it. Specific beats clever.
6. **Treat the OP as a peer.** Not a lead, not a customer, not a student. No advice they didn't ask for. No "you should".
7. **Tease the tool, the demo or the hype, never the person.** Self-owns are always safe ("we've spent longer arguing about a logo colour").
8. **Facts only when earned** (see section 5). When you do use one, it goes in plain words, no link, no "heads up", and you agree with the person first.
9. **No pitch in other people's threads.** No link to our site in someone else's thread unless they asked "where can I see this?".
10. **Post once, then leave.** In a heated thread we never go back to argue. One reply, then go.
11. **Corrections:** if someone corrects us, say so plainly and thank them. Tripo's "We just took the post down. We're really sorry…" (L53) did 25× better than its usual cheerful replies.

## 3. How we post (originals)

1. **Ride a moment we have a real claim on.** A launch, an engine or Steam policy change, a viral argument about AI in games. MoonPie's best post rode the Artemis moon photos (L12,044) because the moon is literally their brand. Ours is "which tool should I use for this, and what's new?". A launch day is our Artemis.
2. **~140 characters, one idea, no hashtags, no link in the post.** The one link goes in the first reply.
3. **Lowercase for personality posts, Title Case only for fake Onion headlines.** The Onion's "Zillow Adds Segregation Score" is four words (L1,472).
4. **Joke in the post, fact in the first reply.** The post should work for someone who never opens the reply. The reply is the receipt: one dated fact and the link.
5. **About 60% bits, 40% useful.** The useful posts still sound like us.
6. **Quote-posts:** only for vendor launches and friendly showcases where our line adds something. Never quote-post a dev to correct or dunk on them. Never quote a hostile pile-on.

## 3a. The pinned intro and profile

The pinned post is the account's explanation for anyone who lands from a reply. It should:
- say in the first line what we're for: comparing the latest AI tools for game dev, by job.
- name the jobs people search for (3D, textures, voice, music, coding) so they recognise their problem.
- mention the three things on the site: side-by-side comparisons, what's new, practical guides. Pricing and terms come as a column, not the headline.
- carry one live-site number at most, copied exactly from the page it's on.
- keep the link in the first reply, never in the post. It can run to about 200 characters because it's read, not scrolled past.

Don't lead the pinned post with licences, "terms of service" or "we read the fine print". That was the old identity.

## 4. Sentences and punctuation

- Lowercase by default. Capitals for acronyms only when lowercase would confuse ("P" in "with a P" is fine, "ai" is fine lowercase).
- Short sentences. Full stops are fine. Commas are fine.
- **No em dashes. No semicolons.** Use a full stop or a comma.
- No exclamation marks in replies. In posts, one at most, rarely.
- Quote marks only around words someone actually wrote, copied exactly. Otherwise paraphrase with no quote marks.
- Digits for numbers ("14 of the 43", "3 days").
- Emoji: none by default, one at most. Never 🚀 🔥 🙌 👇 🧵.
- No hashtags. No @-tagging people who aren't in the thread.
- Australian spelling where a word has one: licence (noun), localisation, colour, realise, organise, favourite.
- Don't start replies with "So," or "Honestly," every time. Vary it or just start.

## 5. Words

**We use:** reckon, a bit, the lot, fair, for now, yeah, roughly, side by side, stacks up, for the job, for props / for characters / for NPC lines, worth a look, we tried, we checked, dated, the catch, the bit that matters, mate (rarely), heaps (rarely).

**Retire as identity lines** (fine as an occasional fact, not as who we are): "we read the terms so you don't have to", "the terms page", "the boring bit", "paperwork desk".

**We never use:**
- heads up / heads-up, PSA, just a reminder, quick reminder, friendly reminder, fun fact
- great question, love this, this is huge, massive, insane, wild, the real unlock, unlock, game-changer / game changer, next-level, level up
- dive in, deep dive, excited to, thrilled, delighted, can't wait to see
- leverage, empower, elevate, supercharge, seamless, robust, revolutionise, the future of
- check out, don't sleep on, follow us for more, let us know, feel free to, we're here to help
- "it's not X, it's Y" as a formula, "not only X but also Y"
- em dashes (—), semicolons, 🚀, 🧵, hashtags

Why: every one of those shows up in the zero-like replies we pulled (section 9). "Love this… game changer" replies from accounts with 30–40k followers got 0 likes. "Heads up" licence replies, which are exactly our old format, got 0–7.

## 6. When a fact belongs in someone's thread

Default: **it doesn't.** A licence fact dropped into a showcase reads as "well actually" and as a pitch. The "heads up: the licence is non-commercial" replies we found got 0 likes (I45–55), and the best one (L7) only worked because it gave a specific workaround.

A fact is earned when:
- **someone asked which tool to use** ("what do people use for concept art to 3D?"). A short comparison is exactly our job. Name two or three tools, the one thing that separates them, and no link.
- **someone asked.** In Scenario's plugin thread a replier asked "Is it OK for commercial use?". That's our spot. Tripo got a direct "do commercial rights apply…?" question. That's a spot.
- **the OP made a claim about the rules.** @ZikadaPrime posted "Vibe coded games don't need to be disclosed under Valve's AI disclosure rules". A short, friendly line with Valve's own wording fits. A correction-toned paragraph doesn't.
- **it's our own post or quote-post.** Facts live there and in our first replies.

A fact is not earned when:
- it's a showcase ("look what I built"). React to the thing.
- it's someone sharing a failure or a hard time. Be kind, or say nothing.
- it's a hostile pile-on (today's Sweeney thread). Stay out and post our own take.

If someone in the thread then asks about licences, a second reply with the fact is fine. Still no link unless they ask where to look.

## 8. Safety rules (carried over from brand-voice.md, these still apply)

**Tone warning:** the community is split and hostile to hype. We punch at hype, demos and vendor claims. We never punch at artists worried about their jobs or at devs who use AI. Steer clear of taking sides in the culture war; be the honest, informed, funny friend.

**Don't**
- Mock artists, beginners, or anyone's game.
- Invent stats, fake quotes from real people, or fake screenshots of real accounts.
- Make claims that defame a named tool ("X steals art"). Joke about tool categories, or about claims a vendor actually made.
- Write "🚀 Introducing…", "game-changer", or thread-bro hooks.
- Engage in political or culture-war dunking. Don't quote-tweet a dev to dunk on them.
- Imply we build AI models or make games ourselves (no "our playtesters", no "our game").
- Put words in anyone's mouth. Paraphrase without quote marks unless it's an exact quote.

**AI images:** generated images (Higgsfield) at most once a week, only when the joke is visual and honestly about AI output. Always label the post or alt text as AI-generated. Never use them to fake a real tool's output, a screenshot, a person or a news event. Alt text on every image.

**Facts:** every number or licence term must match `data/tools.json`, a built page, or the vendor's page exactly, and we say the date we checked. If a vendor or dev corrects us, thank them, fix the site, and say so publicly.

**Never:** DM-baiting, engagement-farming giveaways, arguing with angry users, links in the body of a post.

### Patterns, in one place
- Winners: 3–15 words, lowercase or plain sentence case, one idea, a detail lifted from the OP, a twist or a self-own, then stop.
- Losers: restating the post, ad words (unlock, game-changer, huge), exclamation marks, emoji piles, "heads up" facts nobody asked for, calls to action, hashtags.
- Facts can win, but only when they're asked for or the post is ours.
